Why KWin waited on the NVIDIA driver

Question: on 2026-10-18 the PC froze during the scroll bench and had to be powered off. The journal showed the desktop's KWin stuck inside the NVIDIA driver. Why was it waiting there?

What it was waiting for (established). KWin was applying a display configuration for the monitor that had just come back. While doing that, the driver turned variable refresh rate (VRR) off on the DisplayPort link. That needs a small write over the DP AUX channel to the monitor. On this GPU every such request goes through the GPU's own firmware (GSP), and the firmware never answered. KWin kept polling for the reply while holding the driver's display lock. That blocked every other display operation, and the screen froze. All eight kernel hang reports between 12:04 and 12:18 show this same call, still unanswered.

Why the firmware didn't answer (not established). The evidence points to the GPU firmware having stopped answering requests about 3.5 minutes before KWin hit it, at 11:56:33. That is the moment the bench killed one app and started the next. The new app froze during startup. A second freshly started graphical program, KSplash, also failed to come up at 11:59:58. KWin was only the third victim, and the first to hold a lock the whole display stack needs. Two hypotheses are compared below. One question only you can answer would settle most of it: had the screen already frozen before the monitor went off and on at about 12:00?

1. The call KWin was stuck in

Source: journalctl -b -1 -k, the kernel's hung-task report at 12:04:04. The report names the nvidia-modeset kernel thread as blocked "on a semaphore likely last held by task kwin_wayland:14716". PID 14716 is your desktop's KWin, running since Oct 10, not the bench's nested KWin. Read bottom to top, KWin's kernel stack is:

  • The same call every time. Across the eight reports (blocked 122 s, then 245 s, and so on up to 983 s), nvDisableVrr, setIgnoreMSAEnable and the AUX transaction each appear eight times, and kgspRecvPoll 14 times. KWin never moved on to a different request.
  • KWin was spinning, not sleeping. It was in state R inside the poll loop, which also calls the firmware health check (kgspHealthCheck_TU102) and drains firmware events (_kgspRpcDrainEvents).
  • The driver never gave up or logged an error. There is no NVRM, Xid or "GSP RPC timeout" line anywhere in that boot's journal.
  • Why VRR was being turned off. In ~/.config/kwinoutputconfig.json, DP-1's vrrPolicy is "Automatic". Re-applying its mode after the reconnect went through the driver's VRR-off path. The DPCD bit being written, "ignore MSA timing parameters", is the monitor-side switch for adaptive sync.

3. Why the firmware didn't answer: two hypotheses

A. The GPU firmware stopped answering at about 11:56:33, and KWin was the first program to need it while holding the display lock

  • For: the bench app started at 11:56:33 froze at startup. In gpu-shm mode the web process creates a GPU context and buffers at startup, and on this driver those allocations are firmware requests. Nothing else explains its stall: the cleanup pass had finished (run copy warmup.log: 56,698 cleared of 58,147 at 11:54:52), and the two launches before it reached their first page in about 50 ms.
  • For: KSplash, a QML program that renders on the GPU, started at 11:59:58 and never registered within 60 s. That is a second fresh GPU client failing before KWin's 12:00:21 modeset.
  • For: it fits the moment. 11:56:32-33 is a GPU process being killed mid-work, its GPU resources torn down, and a new one started within a second. The bench does this nine times per run. Run 1 survived eight cycles; run 2 froze on its third.
  • For: screens can keep showing frames without the firmware. Flips go through display channels the CPU writes directly, so the desktop could look alive while new GPU programs hung at startup.
  • Gap: nothing in the journal shows the desktop between 11:56:33 and 11:59:35 (no focus change after 11:52:14), so whether the screen was still updating is unknown.
  • Gap: the driver logged no firmware timeout or error at all, which a dead firmware would normally produce.

B. The monitor dropped the link on its own, and the driver's AUX write to the reconnecting monitor hung

  • For: the stuck call is a DP AUX write to the monitor itself, made 0-40 s after it came back. A QD-OLED monitor coming out of standby may not answer AUX yet. The driver should time out and retry (AuxRetry), but here it never returned.
  • For: monitor reconnects are normal on this machine. DP-1 hotplugged on Oct 16 and at 10:29 today, and KWin recovered both times. At 10:29 it logged GL_OUT_OF_MEMORY four times while recreating its buffers, and still recovered.
  • Against: it doesn't explain the bench app freezing 3 minutes before the monitor dropped, or KSplash failing before KWin's modeset.
  • Against: the idle screen-off is unlikely as the trigger. Power settings turn the display off after 1800 s idle (~/.config/powerdevilrc). The last focus change was 7 minutes earlier, and a playing picture-in-picture video normally inhibits idle. The 11:59:35 drop is also not a suspend: the last suspend in that boot was Oct 16.

Reading: A explains more of the evidence. Under A, the monitor going off at 11:59:35 and back on at 12:00:21 may have been you power-cycling a frozen screen. The USB device on the monitor's hub flapping at 12:00:05-12:00:17 fits that. If the screen was still live when it went dark, B gains weight, and the bench app's stall becomes a separate problem.

5. What this means for the bench

  • GPU modes are the risk. Under hypothesis A, the bench's pattern of killing a GPU-rendering WebKit process and starting another within a second is the most likely trigger. gpu-shm and explicit-sync both render on the GPU. cpu mode paints in software, but the nested KWin still uses the GPU, so it is lower risk, not zero.
  • The app itself is not the problem. Memory stayed far under the cap, and the frozen app did nothing unusual before it froze.
  • Unattended runs turn a GPU stall into a lost session. If the desktop KWin touches the display while the firmware is stuck, the whole machine freezes.

6. What could be done

None of these were tried. They are listed from cheapest to most involved.

  • Settle the question: recall whether the screen had frozen before the monitor went off at about 12:00.
  • Space out bench launches: make scroll-bench.sh wait for the killed app's scope to be gone, plus a few seconds, before starting the next process. This is cheap and targets hypothesis A's trigger. It can't be shown to work without rerunning.
  • Set DP-1's VRR policy to Never: KWin would then stop taking the VRR-off AUX path on reconnect. That removes the exact call that hung, but not whatever stopped the firmware answering.
  • Enable nvidia-persistenced: NVIDIA forum replies to similar GSP hangs recommend it. It is unverified here.
  • Report it upstream: NVIDIA's open-gpu-kernel-modules issues or forum, with the eight stacks and an nvidia-bug-report.sh from a boot where it reproduces. The closest public report found is a different NVKMS lock hang on an RTX 5080 (DIFR prefetch), not this AUX path.
  • Only bench GPU modes while at the machine, so a stall can be caught, for example by switching to a VT, before it freezes the session.

7. What this report did not establish

  • Why the firmware stopped answering. The journal shows that KWin's request got no reply, not why. There is no firmware log: EnableGpuFirmwareLogs output isn't in the journal, and no nvidia-bug-report.sh was taken before the reboot.
  • Where the bench app froze. Its process is gone, and its log stops at "indexing complete", so it is inferred, not shown, that it froze in a GPU call.
  • Whether the screen was live from 11:56:33 to 11:59:35. No journal line shows the desktop rendering or taking input then.
  • The driver's AUX and RPC timeout logic. I didn't read the 615.78.08 source, so I don't know why the poll never timed out.
  • Reproduction. At your request, nothing was rerun, so neither hypothesis has been tested.
  • Web search. No public report names nvDisableVrr, setIgnoreMSAEnable or EvoAuxBus in a hang.
    • Closest: "kwin/wayland complete hang in nvidia-open", an RTX 5080, but the NVKMS lock is held in a different path.
    • GSP hang advice: NVIDIA forum, 570.124.04 hangs.
